
Bro, here's the consolidated summary of our discussion and the architecture we've decided to build.

### AI-Powered Multilingual Video Intelligence – Preprocessing Stage

### 1\. Our main objective

Given a 30-second video at 30 FPS:

* Total frames: 900

* Extract audio separately.

* Identify important visual information.

* Extract all visible burned-in subtitles.

* Reduce redundant frames without losing meaningful information.

* Prepare everything for GPT-4o analysis.

Our architecture has three independent processing tracks.

### 2\. Overall architecture

Input Video

### 30 seconds · 900 frames

Video Preprocessing

FFmpeg + OpenCV · Decode sequentially

Track A Visual frame selection

Select 80–100 representative frames (initial target).

Track B Subtitle OCR

Extract complete burned-in subtitle timeline.

Track C Audio extraction

Extract audio for speech transcription.

Evidence Collection

Visual evidence + subtitle evidence + audio transcript

GPT-4o Analysis

Translation, reconciliation, entities and summaries

The three tracks can operate independently. For your heat-sensitive ThinkPad, we'll initially execute them sequentially or with controlled concurrency.

### 3\. Track A – Visual frame selection

Objective: Reduce 900 frames to a smaller set while preserving meaningful visual information.

Five techniques:

| Technique               | Purpose                                         |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Scene Detection         | Identify scene changes                          |
| Keyframe Selection      | Select representative frames                    |
| pHash / SSIM            | Remove redundant frames                         |
| OCR-assisted protection | Preserve frames containing changing visual text |
| Motion Detection        | Identify meaningful movement                    |

Example:

```text
900 decoded frames
       |
Scene Detection
       |
Keyframe Selection
       |
Duplicate Removal
       |
Motion + Text Protection
       |
80–100 selected frames (illustrative)
```

These selected frames will help identify:

* Medicines and product packaging.

* Charts and graphs.

* Logos and images.

* Medical claims and adverse-effect information.

* Screenshots and chat messages.

* Other important visual elements.

### 4\. Track B – Subtitle extraction

Objective: Extract the complete burned-in subtitle timeline, not just subtitles visible in selected frames.

Process:

* Read the full video timeline.

* Inspect the subtitle region across frames.

* Detect new or changing text.

* Run OCR when appropriate.

* Remove duplicate text observations.

* Preserve original timestamps.

* Generate JSON and SRT files.

Example:

```text
900 frames
    |
Subtitle-region inspection
    |
Text change detection
    |
OCR
    |
Duplicate text handling
    |
Complete subtitle timeline
```

Important: Track B must not depend on Track A's 80–100 selected frames.

We cannot guarantee zero OCR misses, so we'll validate against the original video, particularly for short-lived subtitles.

### 5\. Track C – Audio extraction

Objective: Extract the complete original audio independently of visual frame selection.

```text
Original video
      |
FFmpeg
      |
Extract audio
      |
WAV file
      |
GPT-4o Transcribe
      |
Original-language transcript
      |
GPT-4o translation
      |
English transcript
```

For our Japanese example, we preserve the Japanese transcript and generate its English translation.

English audio generation is excluded.

### 7\. Our first test configuration

| Parameter               | Value                |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- |
| Video duration          | 30 seconds           |
| FPS                     | 30                   |
| Total frames            | 900                  |
| Initial batch size      | 15                   |
| Number of batches       | 60                   |
| Visual frame target     | 80–100               |
| Subtitle coverage       | Full video timeline  |
| Audio extraction        | Full audio           |
| Permanent frame storage | Selected frames only |

The batch size is a starting point. We will benchmark it on the ThinkPad before increasing it.

### 8\. Implementation order

* Video loading and metadata extraction.

* Sequential frame decoding and audio extraction.

* Batch processing.

* Scene detection and keyframe selection.

* Duplicate removal and motion analysis.

* Subtitle OCR and text-change detection.

* Visual OCR on selected frames.

* Integration of all three tracks.

* Testing, accuracy validation and resource monitoring.

### Our final objective

From one video, produce three complete sources of evidence:

* Track A: Important visual frames.

* Track B: Complete visible subtitle timeline.

* Track C: Complete audio and speech transcript.

Then combine these sources for GPT-4o to understand what the speaker says, what the video displays, and whether the audio and visual information differ.

That's the foundation of our Multilingual Video Intelligence Platform. ❤️
